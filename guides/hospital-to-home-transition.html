Planning for a Safe Hospital-to-Home Transition

The care is usually fine. The handoff is what fails. Keep this page in the hospital bag.

Your rights, the two notices to find and keep, and a checklist for the first 72 hours home — the phone number in this guide is the one families wish they'd had.

Two Notices to Find and Keep

Important Message from Medicare

Every inpatient gets it within two days of admission, and again before discharge. It states your right to medically necessary care, to be involved in decisions, and to appeal. The appeal instructions are printed on it.

Medicare Outpatient Observation Notice

If you are under observation rather than admitted, the hospital must give you this in writing within 36 hours and explain it out loud.

Ask on day one, then ask again

“Is he admitted as an inpatient, or under observation?” Observation looks identical from the bed — same room, same gown. But Medicare pays for skilled nursing rehab only after a 3-day inpatient stay, and observation days do not count. Three nights in a hospital bed, then rehab, can arrive as a bill for the entire rehab stay.

New Jersey's CARE Act — What the Hospital Owes You

  • At admission you must be offered the chance to name a caregiver in the record. Do it.
  • That caregiver must be notified by the time the discharge order is written.
  • The hospital must consult you and issue a written discharge plan that names a specific hospital employee you can call afterward.
  • The hospital must teach you the care tasks — plain language, with a demonstration and a chance to ask questions.
“I am the designated caregiver under the New Jersey CARE Act. Please show me how to do this, and give me the written discharge plan with the name of who I call.”

Before You Leave the Building

Names and phone numbers to call with questions — written down
What complications to watch for, and what to do about each
What equipment is needed, whether Medicare covers it, and who is arranging delivery
Written instructions you can actually read, plus a current medication list
The follow-up appointment already booked — not from the car

The 72-Hour Home Plan

Can he get in? Steps, rails, and a clear path to a bed and bathroom — one level if possible
Medications sorted, old bottles physically out of the house
Food, and someone there. The first 72 hours send people back
A phone within reach of the bed, and a plan for the first night
The highest-risk moment of the whole process

Put every bottle from home on the table next to the new prescriptions and ask which ones he stops. Hospitals start drugs, change doses, and substitute — but nobody clears the old bottles off the kitchen counter. Doubled doses and duplicate blood thinners are how people get hurt in week one.

If a Discharge Feels Too Early

You can appeal — free, fast, and you are not billed while it's pending. New Jersey's Medicare discharge appeal organization is Commence Health: 866-815-5440, TTY 711, available 24 hours.

Also ask

The hospital social worker, and the person named on your written discharge plan.
