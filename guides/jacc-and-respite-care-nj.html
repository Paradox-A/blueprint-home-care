How NJ's JACC and Statewide Respite Care Programs Actually Work

Two different state programs, often confused with each other, that can help pay for in-home help or give a family caregiver a break. Here's who qualifies and how to apply.

Jersey Assistance for Community Caregiving (JACC) and the Statewide Respite Care Program (SRCP) both come out of New Jersey's Division of Aging Services, both start with the same phone call, and both get mixed up with each other constantly. They're not the same thing. JACC helps pay for ongoing home care for someone who clinically needs nursing-home-level support. SRCP exists for the unpaid caregiver — it pays for a break, not for everyday care.

Please read before you apply

This page is general background, not a promise of eligibility, an award amount, or legal or financial advice. Income limits, cost-share percentages, and covered services are set by the state and change periodically. Confirm the current rules with your county's Aging & Disability Resource Connection (ADRC) before you plan around any figure here.

What JACC Actually Is

JACC is a state-funded program for New Jersey residents age 60 and older who meet New Jersey's clinical eligibility standard for nursing facility level of care — generally, needing hands-on help with at least three activities of daily living, such as bathing, toileting, dressing, transferring, or getting around — but who want to keep living at home instead of moving to a facility.

That clinical determination isn't self-reported. New Jersey's Office of Community Choice Options (OCCO), or an assessor from a PACE organization or your county ADRC, administers a standardized evaluation called the NJ Choice assessment to decide whether someone meets nursing facility level of care under N.J.A.C. 8:85-2.1. The assessment scores what you actually do for yourself day to day, not what you could theoretically manage.

JACC is specifically for people in this situation who are not already enrolled in Medicaid or NJ FamilyCare's Managed Long Term Services and Supports (MLTSS) program — it's a bridge for people who need that level of care but haven't gone the Medicaid route.

What JACC Covers

Depending on your county and your care plan, JACC can help pay for:

  • Care management to help build and adjust the plan
  • A home health aide or personal care assistance
  • Homemaker-type help, like light housekeeping
  • Home-delivered meals
  • Respite care
  • Home modifications for accessibility and a Personal Emergency Response System (PERS)
  • Chore services and some special medical equipment or supplies

JACC carries an income-based copay rather than a flat fee or a hard income cutoff, and the exact services authorized come out of your individual care plan — not every participant gets every service on this list. Ask your county ADRC what your specific copay and care plan would look like.

What the Statewide Respite Care Program Actually Is

SRCP isn't aimed at the person receiving care — it's built around giving their unpaid caregiver a break. To qualify, the care recipient needs a documented functional impairment and needs to be a New Jersey resident cared for by an uncompensated caregiver age 18 or older (most commonly a family member).

Covered respite options can include adult day care, an in-home respite aide, companion services, a brief overnight or short-term facility stay, or a supervised camp-style respite program, depending on what your county's program offers and what fits your situation.

What SRCP Costs

SRCP uses a sliding-scale cost share, from 0% up to 25% of the cost of services, based on the care recipient's (and spouse's, if married) income. As of 2026, New Jersey's published program guidance lists a monthly gross income limit of $2,982 for an individual and $5,964 for a couple, with asset limits of $40,000 and $60,000 respectively — but these figures are reviewed and adjusted by the state, so verify the current numbers with your county ADRC rather than relying on this page for an exact figure by the time you apply.

There's also a limit on how much respite service the program will cover per person per year. Ask your county program coordinator for the current annual limit when you apply — it's set and adjusted at the program level rather than published in one place families can easily check themselves.

How to Apply to Either Program

1.Call New Jersey's Aging & Disability Resource Connection at 1-877-222-3737, or contact your county's Area Agency on Aging/ADRC office directly. One call covers screening for both JACC and SRCP, along with other state aging programs.
2.For JACC, expect a NJ Choice functional assessment to establish nursing facility level of care. Gather a list of current diagnoses, medications, and the daily tasks your loved one needs help with before that appointment.
3.For SRCP, be ready to document the care recipient's functional impairment and the caregiving relationship, plus household income and asset information for the cost-share calculation.
4.Ask directly what your county's current income limits, copay amounts, and service caps are — they vary by program and change periodically, and your ADRC intake worker will have the current numbers.
5.If you're turned down or told you don't qualify, ask why in writing. Clinical level-of-care denials can be appealed, and an ADRC worker or a county veteran/aging caseworker can tell you what that process looks like for your situation.
